Playing against the AI

No opponent around at 3 a.m.? The computer is always awake. Four levels, from a gentle teacher to an opponent that out-counts almost every human — and practice games never touch your rating, so every experiment is free.

▶ Challenge the AI

The four levels

🙂 Easy — learn the moves

Plays the strong move about a third of the time and a casual move the rest. Wins roughly one game in five against a beginner who knows the rules. Use it for your very first games and for testing a brand-new idea without pressure.

😎 Normal — a fair fight

Picks the strong move about half the time. Beats a casual player roughly every second game. This is the level to measure yourself against: when you beat Normal consistently, you are ready for real opponents.

🔥 Hard — punishes mistakes

Plays the strong move two times in three and wins about four games in five against average players. Hanging pawns, wasted walls and lazy endgames all get punished. Beat Hard regularly and you will hold your own in public rooms.

💀 Hardcore — the real engine

No holding back: a full game-tree search that thinks for over a second every move, examining your replies to every line and playing only into positions it can hold. It practically cannot be beaten — play it to find the holes in your game, not to pad your ego.

How the computer thinks

Honestly, and the same way this guide teaches you to think. Every move it measures both shortest paths — yours and its own — and weighs walls by how much they lengthen your road. Its wall candidates cluster around your shortest path and extend existing structures at choke points, exactly like the wall tactics page recommends. The lower levels simply mix in weaker moves on purpose; Hardcore searches many moves deep and never blunders. If you want to understand why it beat you, replay the game: the endgame math section shows how to count what it counted.

How to train on each level

  • New to the game: Easy until the rules feel automatic — movement, jumps, what a legal wall looks like.
  • Learning strategy: Normal while working through path counting and tempo. Try one new idea per game.
  • Before going public: Hard until you win at least half your games. Public opponents play roughly at this level.
  • Plateaued? Lose ten games to Hardcore and replay each one. You will see exactly which walls were wasted and which runs were mistimed.

Tips to beat the computer

  • Count, don't feel. The AI always knows both path lengths. Match it: count every turn and you remove its biggest edge.
  • Punish its honesty. Lower levels play weak moves on purpose — when it drifts, take the centre and make it pay before it recovers.
  • Endgames are yours. Against Easy through Hard, precise endgame counting wins games: stop walling early and run the numbers.
  • Against Hardcore, aim small. Don't try to out-wall it — keep your road short, force jumps, and take the one mistake it never makes twice.

Good to know

  • AI games never affect points, rank or the leaderboard — win or lose, your rating doesn't move.
  • The AI plays by the exact same rules as you: same walls, same clock, same refused illegal walls.
  • Stuck? The FAQ answers rule edge cases, and we answer everything else.
